Hidden treasures

Beads are the heart of every trip, but Czechia has many other hidden treasures — historic towns, remarkable churches and quiet old corners that are well worth a day out.

Český Krumlov, Telč, Tábor, Cheb, Mariánské Lázně, Kutná Hora and Jablonec — and a Gothic crypt.

Český Krumlov

A small town in South Bohemia, wrapped around a bend in the Vltava and watched over by its castle. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Kutná Hora

Once a wealthy silver-mining town, Kutná Hora is known for St Barbara’s Church, a striking Gothic church, and its historic centre is also UNESCO-listed.

Telč, Tábor, Cheb and Mariánské Lázně

Telč, in southern Moravia, is known for its long square of arcaded Renaissance and Baroque houses, and its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Tábor, in South Bohemia, was founded by the Hussites in the 15th century; its old town is a maze of narrow lanes around Žižka Square.

Cheb, in the far west near the German border, has a sloping main square lined with colourful burgher houses, including the half-timbered Špalíček.

Mariánské Lázně, one of the West Bohemian spa towns, is known for its elegant cast-iron Colonnade from the late 19th century, and is part of the UNESCO-listed Great Spa Towns of Europe.
