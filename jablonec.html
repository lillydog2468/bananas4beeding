Jablonec

A hillside town in northern Bohemia, and for centuries one of the great homes of Czech glass and beads.

Where it is

Jablonec nad Nisou sits in the Jizera Mountains, about an hour and a half from Prague. It is a proper town with a square, tram lines, cafés — and, if you know where to look, beads everywhere.

A short glass story

Glassmaking grew up in these mountains centuries ago, drawn by wood for the furnaces and good sand. By the 1700s and 1800s the area around Jablonec (once known as Gablonz) had become famous for beads, buttons and costume jewellery shipped all over the world.

Small family workshops and larger factories sat side by side. Pressed beads, cut beads, fire-polished glass, lamp-work, waxed pearls — many of the techniques bead lovers still seek out today were refined here.

Why bead people still come

The industry has changed, but the craft has not disappeared. You can still visit makers, poke through antique shops, walk into the Museum of Glass and Jewellery, and watch glass being pressed, cut and lamp-worked.

That is why we base our holidays here. It is not a theme park. It is a living bead town.

The museum

The Museum of Glass and Jewellery is the best place to start if you want the story in one visit — how Czech bead and glass-making grew from the 18th century onward, and how Jablonec became a name bead people still recognise.
