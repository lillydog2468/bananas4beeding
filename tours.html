Tours

A typical tour includes 1 or 2 nights in Prague and 3 or 4 nights in Jablonec.

You’ll be picked up from Prague Airport by Keith (an Englishman living in the Czech Republic) and taken to either your Prague hotel or Jablonec — whichever you choose to visit first.

Prague city centre is about 15 minutes from the airport. Jablonec is about a 1.5-hour drive.

Arriving in Jablonec

Jablonec is a very old, beautiful town — and most importantly, it’s full of beads if you know where to look.

The first thing we do is settle into the hotel, your base for the rest of the week. It’s a nice family-run spot with a restaurant and a function room — perfect for afternoons beading or chatting.

OWNER TO ADD: family-run hotel / pension in Jablonec

Beading afternoons

A lesson is a good way to rest your legs and have some creative fun. It takes place in a room at the back of a shop, with plenty of colours and shapes for a hands-on tutorial.

OWNER TO ADD: workshop / lesson at the back of a shop

Museum of Glass and Jewellery

One of the finest places to visit is the Museum of Glass and Jewellery in Jablonec. It’s a good starting point for the history of Czech bead and glass-making, which dates back to the 18th century.

OWNER TO ADD: Museum of Glass and Jewellery, Jablonec

Antique bead shops

Treasures await in these shops — if you don’t mind getting your hands a little dirty. Some beads were hidden in chimney breasts in times of crisis.

OWNER TO ADD: antique bead shop

Shopping

You’ll find loads of brilliant shops if you know where to look. We spread the shopping through the week so it isn’t too overwhelming. Guests often ask to revisit their favourites towards the end of the trip, which is never a problem.

Production and factories

We visit at least one factory to watch bead production from start to finish — press, polish, cut and fire-polish, all up close. We’ll also tour a glass-waxed pearl factory, which has its own outlet shop. If you’re interested in ordering large quantities, beads can even be made to order.

OWNER TO ADD: factory — press, polish, cut, fire-polish

Lamp-work workshop

We visit a lamp-work bead factory. You can watch a group of very experienced ladies who make it look so easy — and you can have a go yourself. Then you realise that even making a colourful blob of glass is not easy.

They have a beautiful collection of lamp-work beads to choose from, and a fantastic collection of jewellery.

OWNER TO ADD: lamp-work workshop

Christmas glass pearls

The Czech Republic has a rich history of Christmas decorations, dating back to 1800. We’ll visit a spot where small glass pearls are still made the same way they were 100 years ago. You can join a workshop to make your own decorations using these hollow glass pearls.

Glass factory

A glass factory dating back to 1712. You can get close to the glass production on a tour, and see glass cutting how it used to be done before China started to mass-produce it.

OWNER TO ADD: glass factory

Glass button pressing

We’ll visit a small, very old glass button press — still in action — and then have the chance to buy fantastic hand-painted glass buttons, some vintage, some new. This is one of the last button-pressing masters still at work.

Meals together

There are plenty of good places to eat, offering fantastic value. We’ll dine together every evening, either at the hotel restaurant or one of our favourite local spots. Czech cooking is rich and full of flavour. Menus aren’t always tailored to vegetarians, but staff are often very accommodating.

During the whole holiday we are with you in the day, and we spend the evenings with you as well, having good meals out together.

Completely flexible

There is so much to see that 4 or 5 days sometimes isn’t enough. We have guests coming for the third, fourth and fifth time; one traveller even managed to visit twice in one year.

By the end of the tour you will look at your beloved beads with a new-found passion and an appreciation of all the work that goes into making them.

Our holidays are completely flexible. This page is meant to give you an idea of the possibilities. Repeat guests are very welcome.
